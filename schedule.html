Giulia Vilone

From Explanations to Explainability Mediation: LLMs, XAI, and the Regulatory Future of Human-Centered AI

Abstract

AI systems are increasingly embedded in high-stakes decisions. Thus, explainability is no longer only a methodological challenge; it is becoming a socio-technical and regulatory requirement. Traditional XAI methods provide valuable technical artefacts, such as feature attributions, counterfactuals, saliency maps, surrogate models and logs, that could help understand an opaque model. However, these artefacts are often difficult to interpret for deployers, supervisors and, more so, affected individuals with limited literacy in AI. Moreover, as pointed out by various studies, few researchers pay attention to the last mile problem of XAI systems, i.e. the presentation of explanations to end-users in such a way as to be comprehensible, effective and trustworthy.

The recent emergence of Large Language Models (LLMs) opens a new possibility: using LLMs as translators of the technical artefacts into natural-language, interactive and audience-specific explanations. LLMs can help overcome the last mile of XAI systems by making explanations more accessible, contextual and usable, particularly for non-expert stakeholders. On the negative side, LLMs may introduce hallucinations, misleading simplifications, ambiguous narratives and a gap between plausibility and faithfulness to the model’s inferential process. Such a gap could be dangerous; human users could struggle to detect errors in convincing explanations. In AI-supported decision-making processes, LLM-generated explanations can also increase over-trust in AI recommendations rather than support appropriate reliance.

Overcoming these scientific challenges has become a legal requirement under the emerging European regulatory landscape, particularly the AI Act. The AI Act requires transparency for high-risk AI-based systems and the provision of information that enables deployers to interpret outputs, employers to perform effective human oversight in specific cases, and affected persons to exercise their right to clear and meaningful explanations of AI recommendations. Besides the AI Act, the GDPR demands a right to explanation and the people’s privacy, thus forbidding explanations that reveal sensitive data. XAI explanations must also take into account the risks of disclosing patent-protected information or trade secrets and jeopardising a company’s business.

Conclusions will outline a research agenda for LLM-mediated XAI explanations that must account for legal constraints and be adaptable to the cognitive processes and knowledge background of the various stakeholders of AI-based systems. Additionally, it is vital to develop evaluation frameworks that jointly measure fidelity, understandability, usefulness, privacy and regulatory compliance in an objective, universal manner.

Short Bio

Giulia Vilone is a Senior ML Engineer at Analog Devices International. Following her PhD on XAI and argumentation, she investigates real-world applications of XAI in human–machine interaction and the integration of technical and legal perspectives on explainability. Her research focuses on Edge AI, Computer Vision, Argumentation, Explainable AI, and Legal Compliance.

Flavio Giorgi
Invited Speaker
Postdoctoral Researcher, Sapienza University of Rome
Co-Founder, CEO & CTO, TellmewAI

Faithful Is Not Enough: XAI Narratives from the Lab to Real-World Deployment

Abstract

Large Language Models (LLMs) can transform the technical artifacts produced by XAI methods, such as feature attributions and counterfactual explanations, into natural-language narratives that people can actually read. However, the fluency of a narrative does not guarantee its faithfulness to the underlying model. A convincing explanation may cite features the model never relied on, omit the ones it did, or even contradict the very artifact it is supposed to describe, and the reader has little chance of noticing. The question, therefore, is not whether LLMs can explain, but under which conditions they can act as reliable mediators between a model and the people affected by its decisions.

This talk argues that an LLM can play this role only when three conditions hold. First, the narrative must be grounded in an explicit XAI artifact, rather than produced by directly prompting the model for a post-hoc rationale. Second, it must be refined, so that inconsistencies and errors across alternative drafts are identified and resolved. Third, it must be verified against the original artifact before reaching the end-user. These conditions are illustrated through a line of research that starts from natural-language counterfactual explanations for graph neural networks, where LLMs verbalize counterfactual artifacts instead of reasoning freely about a decision. It then continues with a multi-narrative draft-and-refine pipeline, in which knowledge distillation enables small language models to generate coherent counterfactual narratives, whose consistency with the factual and counterfactual ground truth can be assessed automatically.

Moving from research to real-world deployment changes the nature of these conditions. At TellmewAI, a Compliant-AI-as-a-Service platform, grounding, refinement, and verification are no longer design choices but requirements stemming from the EU AI Act and from standards such as ISO/IEC 42001 and ISO/IEC 42005. Grounding becomes traceability, as each statement of a narrative must be linked to the artifact that supports it. Refinement becomes an internal control over the generation process, and verification becomes evidence that can be preserved and presented during an audit. Ultimately, the experience of TellmewAI shows that organizations need explanations tailored to each role, from data scientists to compliance officers and auditors, that remain valid as models are retrained and that can be traced and defended during an inspection. They also need explanations generated where sensitive data reside, which small language models make possible without moving data outside the organization. Meeting these needs at scale is what turns XAI narratives from a research result into a compliance product.

Short Bio

Flavio Giorgi is a Postdoctoral Researcher in the Department of Computer Science at Sapienza University of Rome and the Co-Founder, CEO, and CTO of TellmewAI, a Compliant-AI-as-a-Service platform for EU AI Act compliance. He received his PhD in Computer Science from Sapienza and was a visiting PhD student at the Technical University of Munich. His research focuses on Explainable AI, Graph Neural Networks, counterfactual explanations, and Large Language Models.
